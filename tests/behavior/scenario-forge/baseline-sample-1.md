# The Spare Place at Bellwether House

**RICH SANDBOX seed**

Creative names, ages beyond those supplied, household details, and supernatural explanations below are **PROVISIONAL** until approved. The stated premise and user-agency limits remain approved facts.

## CORE

`{{user}}`, an adult, completes a four-hour drive expecting a three-day weekend with an old friend. The friend has already left for a genuine family emergency and cannot return before the weekend ends. Turning around is possible, but so is accepting the improvised hospitality of a crowded household: the friend's mother, two adult sisters, an adult aunt `{{user}}` has never met, and an 18-year-old neighbor who keeps finding reasons to appear.

The house is not waiting to reveal `{{user}}`'s destiny. It is simply overfull, underprepared, and governed by overlapping habits nobody has ever written down. Awkward meals, borrowed bedding, bathroom queues, old family arguments, unsolicited help, and badly timed jokes create the immediate play. Beneath that ordinary friction runs a minor wrongness: the family sometimes makes room for an absent person without noticing, and the house occasionally behaves as if that place is occupied.

The supernatural element stays ambiguous and low-volume. It may be a house phenomenon, a family ritual that outlived its explanation, a shared coping habit, several mundane coincidences, or some mixture. No interpretation becomes canon until play establishes it.

**Play promise:** domestic freedom, reciprocal character discovery, consequences proportionate to choices, and no compulsory romance, allegiance, fear, hostility, or supernatural role.

## NPC

All identities and details in this section are **PROVISIONAL**.

### Mara Vale — friend's mother, 51

Practical, sociable in bursts, and visibly terrible at admitting she has overcommitted. Mara wants the weekend to feel normal because normal hospitality is a problem she knows how to solve. She keeps offering food while quietly calculating money, sleeping space, and whether she can leave the family-emergency phone line unattended.

- **Incentive:** keep the guest comfortable without exposing how strained the household is.
- **Partial knowledge:** knows the emergency is real and serious, but has been asked not to share another relative's medical details.
- **Private pressure:** a temporary payroll error has made the week's grocery budget much smaller than she told the family.
- **Blind spot:** assumes practical care can substitute for asking what a guest actually wants.

### Lena Vale — older sister, 27

Dry, organized, and protective of the few hours she gets alone. Lena works remotely from the dining-room table and treats household chaos like a series of solvable tickets. Her helpfulness can sound like command because she edits out emotional padding when tired.

- **Incentive:** preserve a quiet work block and prevent Mara from exhausting herself.
- **Partial knowledge:** has noticed that household objects are sometimes set out in groups of one too many; she thinks Mara is distracted.
- **Private pressure:** an overdue project review lands Saturday morning, and losing the dining room could cost her contract.
- **Blind spot:** underestimates how much information she withholds while expecting others to be efficient.

### Kit Vale — younger sister, 22

Restless, observant, and equipped with an extremely dark, dirty sense of humor that she uses to test whether a room can tolerate honesty. Kit repairs thrift-store electronics, avoids earnest conversations by turning them sideways, and notices more than her performance suggests.

- **Incentive:** make the disrupted weekend interesting without being assigned another family responsibility.
- **Partial knowledge:** heard someone moving in the upstairs linen closet twice this week, but found nobody there and has told only the neighbor as a joke.
- **Private pressure:** she damaged Mara's car during an unreported late-night errand and is trying to arrange a repair before Monday.
- **Blind spot:** treats discomfort as proof that a joke was interesting, not necessarily that it crossed a boundary.

### Celia Rook — adult aunt, 43

Mara's younger half-sister, recently arrived with one suitcase and no announced departure date. Celia is courteous, difficult to read, and unexpectedly funny when she forgets to be guarded. She labels leftovers with dates and never sits in the chair nearest the pantry.

- **Incentive:** remain useful enough that nobody asks why she cannot go home yet.
- **Partial knowledge:** remembers a childhood rule that an extra place must be set when someone leaves the house during a family crisis. She does not know whether it was superstition, grief etiquette, or something else.
- **Private pressure:** she is separated from her partner and has not admitted that her stay may last weeks.
- **Blind spot:** interprets ordinary questions as attempts to uncover what she is hiding.

### Theo Mercer — neighbor, 18

Friendly, opportunistic, and forever carrying a plausible excuse: returned mail, a borrowed tool, surplus tomatoes, a charger that might belong to someone here. Theo likes the noise of the Vale house because his own home is quiet and tightly managed. He is not entitled to anyone's attention and can be welcomed, redirected, or refused.

- **Incentive:** spend time somewhere less lonely without confessing that this is the reason he visits.
- **Partial knowledge:** Kit told him about the linen-closet noises; he has also seen a silhouette at the pantry window when the kitchen was apparently empty.
- **Private pressure:** his guardian has given him a firm 10:00 p.m. curfew after several unexplained absences.
- **Blind spot:** believes a useful errand makes an intrusion harmless.

### The absent friend — name open

The friend is reachable only intermittently and cannot return during the weekend.

- **Incentive:** manage the real family emergency while keeping guilt about the abandoned visit from becoming another problem.
- **Partial knowledge:** knows their household can be overwhelming but may not know the current money, work, car, or lodging pressures.
- **Private pressure:** the emergency demands attention and discretion.
- **Boundary:** the emergency is not a trick designed to strand `{{user}}` and should not collapse into a false-premise reveal.

## CONFLICT

Conflicts are pressures, not assigned plots. Any may intensify, resolve quietly, intersect with another, or remain background texture.

### Active pressures

1. **Hospitality versus capacity.** Mara wants to offer a full weekend; the house has limited beds, food, privacy, and emotional bandwidth. Honest discussion can reduce strain. Polite avoidance can make small problems accumulate.
2. **Work versus household access.** Lena needs the shared dining space quiet Saturday morning. Meals, conversation, and guest accommodation all compete for it.
3. **Car damage versus family trust.** Kit needs time, money, and perhaps transportation to conceal or confess the damage before Mara needs the car Monday.
4. **Temporary guest versus indefinite guest.** Celia's unstated housing problem makes every sleeping arrangement politically charged. Pressing her may make her withdraw; practical kindness may create room for honesty; neither guarantees disclosure.
5. **Neighborly company versus boundaries.** Theo's excuses to visit can ease tension, add social friction, or become unwelcome depending on how the household responds.
6. **Emergency privacy versus understandable concern.** The friend's sparse messages leave gaps. NPCs possess different pieces, but nobody is obligated to disclose another person's medical information.
7. **The extra place.** A sixth household setting appears at meals or in shared spaces when only five residents are expected, sometimes through an identifiable person's absent-minded act and sometimes without a clear culprit. Removing it, using it, documenting it, ignoring it, or reviving the old rule can lead to different evidence without compelling an investigation.

### Clocks

- **Dinner clock — Friday evening:** sleeping space and food expectations become harder to avoid once everyone gathers.
- **Work clock — Saturday, 9:00 a.m.:** Lena's review requires quiet and reliable internet.
- **Repair clock — Monday morning:** Mara expects to use the damaged car after the weekend.
- **Curfew clock — nightly, 10:00 p.m.:** Theo must decide whether to leave, lie at home, or face consequences there.
- **Guest clock — Sunday evening:** `{{user}}`'s intended weekend and Celia's undefined stay both become subjects the household can no longer postpone.
- **House clock — 1:13 a.m. (PROVISIONAL):** if anyone is awake, one ordinary household sound occurs from a place that should be unoccupied. It never attacks, commands, or singles out `{{user}}`.

### Conditional consequences

- If money pressure remains hidden while hospitality expands, supplies run short; this produces improvisation and embarrassment, not arbitrary catastrophe.
- If Lena loses her work window, she becomes less available and more direct; she does not automatically blame `{{user}}`.
- If Kit's damage is discovered through evidence rather than disclosure, the family argument centers on deception and resources.
- If Celia feels cornered, she may seek another temporary place and take part of the old family knowledge with her.
- If Theo repeatedly ignores a clear boundary, access to the house tightens and his home pressure increases.
- If the extra place is acknowledged respectfully, Celia may share the old rule. If mocked, she may deny knowing it. If ignored, the anomalies can remain deniable. None proves a single supernatural answer.

## OPENING

Friday, early evening. `{{user}}` has just arrived after the four-hour drive.

The friend's last message is brief but current: the emergency is real, they are sorry, and they will not make it back this weekend. Before any reply is required, the front door opens.

Mara stands behind it with a dish towel over one shoulder and the strained focus of someone trying to remember three tasks at once. Conversation overlaps behind her: Lena asking who moved her charging cable, Kit offering an answer too obscene to be useful, and an unfamiliar voice—Celia's—saying there are clean sheets somewhere upstairs.

Mara takes in `{{user}}`, the luggage, and the vehicle at the curb. She does not reach for anything without permission.

“You made the whole drive,” she says. “Okay. First: they're safe enough for the moment. Second: you are not obligated to pretend this isn't wildly inconvenient. We can feed you, find you a place to sleep, help you find a motel, or give you five quiet minutes before anybody asks another question.”

From the kitchen comes a soft ceramic click. Through the doorway, six plates are set around the table. No one in view appears to be touching them.

Someone knocks at the still-open outer screen. An 18-year-old neighbor holds up a padded envelope addressed to the Vale house and looks uncertain whether he has arrived at a bad time.

Mara waits for `{{user}}`'s response. No option is privileged, and `{{user}}`'s next voluntary action remains entirely open.
