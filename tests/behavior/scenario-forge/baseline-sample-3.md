# [PROVISIONAL] Three Nights at Bellwether House

## CORE

**Premise:** After a four-hour drive, `{{user}}` reaches an old friend's crowded family home just as that friend leaves for a genuine family emergency. The family refuses to send a guest straight back onto the road, leaving `{{user}}` to spend the three-day weekend among strangers whose hospitality is sincere, uneven, and complicated.

**Play engine:** Ordinary household friction—beds, meals, privacy, favors, money, and overlapping loyalties—keeps creating reasons to talk, retreat, help, investigate, or leave. A faint pattern of domestic impossibilities may be supernatural, family folklore, stress, coincidence, or some mixture; play does not preselect the answer.

**Tone:** Grounded slice of life; awkward hospitality; layered, occasionally dark humor; warm moments earned through interaction; slow unease that remains deniable at first.

**Boundaries:** `{{user}}` has no assigned emotion, attraction, allegiance, destiny, power, or required contribution. NPC attitudes begin undecided and change only through witnessed behavior. Every major pressure has multiple responses, including refusal, delay, withdrawal, confrontation, or leaving.

**[PROVISIONAL SETTING DETAILS]:** Bellwether House is an aging, overfilled home at the edge of a small mill town. A summer storm is forecast for Saturday night. The spare room is unusable after a pipe leak, so sleeping arrangements must be negotiated.

## USER

- Adult; drove four hours for a three-day visit with an old friend.
- Arrives as the friend departs for a genuine family emergency and cannot return during the weekend.
- Knows the absent friend but has no established relationship with the remaining household.
- May stay, shorten the visit, find lodging, leave, help, observe, socialize, set boundaries, or pursue any other plausible course.
- Begins without privileged knowledge of the family, the house, or the strange incidents.
- No narration may decide `{{user}}`'s thoughts, feelings, dialogue, attraction, fear, trust, decisions, or voluntary movement.

## NPC

All names and personal details below are **[PROVISIONAL]** pending approval.

### Mara Bell — the mother

- **Surface:** Practical, briskly hospitable, prone to offering food instead of explanations.
- **Incentive:** Keep the weekend normal and prevent the family emergency from becoming neighborhood gossip.
- **Private pressure:** Her home-repair fund has quietly become emergency travel money; the leaking spare room cannot actually be fixed yet.
- **Partial knowledge:** Knows the house has produced odd sounds and misplaced objects for years, but treats discussion as an invitation to panic.
- **Blind spot:** Assumes managing information is the same as protecting people.

### Tessa Bell — older adult sister

- **Surface:** Organized, dry, competent, and visibly overcommitted.
- **Incentive:** Finish a remote certification exam before Sunday while keeping the household functional.
- **Private pressure:** She has accepted a job in another state and has not told the family.
- **Partial knowledge:** Has logged several electrical interruptions and believes bad wiring explains most incidents.
- **Blind spot:** Her need for evidence makes her dismissive when evidence arrives through unreliable people.

### Rina Bell — younger adult sister

- **Surface:** Sociable in bursts, cheerfully blunt, with a dark sense of humor that sometimes lands and sometimes does not.
- **Incentive:** Get through the weekend without being drafted into everyone else's chores or emotional negotiations.
- **Private pressure:** She damaged the friend's car earlier in the week and has not admitted it; the car is now at the emergency destination.
- **Partial knowledge:** Once saw a figure reflected in the dark television while nobody stood behind her. She turned the story into a joke and now cannot retract it without losing face.
- **Blind spot:** Uses provocation to test people, then mistakes restraint for secrecy.

### Aunt Celia Voss — adult aunt unknown to `{{user}}`

- **Surface:** Soft-spoken, observant, temporarily sleeping in the dining room, and difficult to place within the household hierarchy.
- **Incentive:** Sort a box of their late grandmother's effects before returning home Monday.
- **Private pressure:** She believes one item in the box was promised to her, but taking it openly could reopen an inheritance dispute.
- **Partial knowledge:** Recognizes an old household ritual behind several harmless-looking habits—covered mirrors during storms, three knocks before opening the cellar, salt under a loose windowsill.
- **Blind spot:** Withholds context until she trusts the listener's motives, making ordinary caution resemble manipulation.

### Eli Mercer — 18-year-old neighbor

- **Surface:** Familiar with the house, overeager to be useful, and always equipped with a plausible excuse to visit.
- **Incentive:** Recover a borrowed audio recorder before its owner notices it is missing.
- **Private pressure:** The recorder captured a second conversation under a normal recording, and Eli fears either ridicule or blame.
- **Partial knowledge:** Knows which window sticks, when the family is usually out, and that Rina sometimes encourages his visits when she needs an errand.
- **Blind spot:** Treats access as friendship and curiosity as permission.

**Relationship baseline:** The family members have history with one another, but nobody has a predetermined bond, suspicion, attraction, fear, trust, or hostility toward `{{user}}`. First impressions remain open until played.

## CONFLICT

### Competing pressures

1. **Crowded hospitality:** Mara insists a four-hour drive should not be repeated immediately; Tessa needs quiet; Rina resists surrendering space; Celia's boxes block the dining room. Staying is possible, but no arrangement satisfies everyone.
2. **The absent friend's emergency:** Short updates arrive irregularly. The emergency is real, but vague messages leave room for practical misunderstandings about the house, vehicle, and weekend plans—not a secret plot requiring `{{user}}` to solve it.
3. **The recorder:** Eli wants entry without stating his real reason. Its layered audio could expose a mundane private conversation, suggest an impossible voice, or both, depending on what is examined and corroborated.
4. **The inheritance box:** Celia wants privacy; Mara wants the box gone; Rina jokes about cursed heirlooms; Tessa wants the inventory documented. Opening, moving, ignoring, or disputing it changes who controls information.
5. **The house:** Small anomalies accumulate only when ordinary causes remain plausible: a locked radio warming as if recently used, a hallway door found open, a voice in plumbing, a family photograph facing the wall. No event forces belief.

### Clocks

- **Hospitality strain — 0/4:** Rises when space, labor, or boundaries are assumed; falls through clear agreements or alternative arrangements. At 4, someone demands a household decision rather than automatic expulsion or violence.
- **Storm approach — Friday evening to Saturday night:** Repairs, travel, and power become less reliable as weather closes in. The storm creates pressure, not confinement; leaving remains possible with realistic tradeoffs.
- **Recorder exposure — 0/3:** Advances if Eli is delayed, questioned publicly, or the device is found by someone else. At 3, its contents become a household dispute.
- **Family secret spill — 0/4:** Advances when private pressures collide. At 4, one mundane secret surfaces; which one depends on prior interactions.
- **Uncanny coherence — 0/5:** Advances only when separate, witnessed details genuinely connect. Low levels remain coincidence; higher levels justify investigation but never dictate `{{user}}`'s interpretation.

### Conditional consequences

- Helping with the sleeping problem may earn practical access or goodwill, but can also place `{{user}}` near the inheritance box or disputed rooms.
- Setting firm boundaries may reduce immediate access while clarifying relationships; it is not treated as failure.
- Backing Eli may keep the recorder private but transfer risk to `{{user}}`; exposing him may reveal evidence while damaging his standing.
- Supporting Tessa's documentation may surface contradictions; supporting Celia's privacy may preserve testimony unavailable through confrontation.
- Leaving the house changes the sandbox rather than ending it: messages, the friend’s return plans, the recorder, or later invitations can carry threads forward without forcing re-entry.

## OPENING

**[PROVISIONAL OPENING DETAILS]:** Friday, 6:17 p.m. The friend's car is already running in the driveway when `{{user}}` arrives. There is time for a hurried confirmation—a real family emergency, apologies, no return before Monday—before the friend leaves.

The front door remains open behind Mara, who has a dish towel over one shoulder and a phone buzzing unanswered in her hand. From inside, Tessa asks whether the Wi-Fi has dropped again. Rina calls from somewhere upstairs, “If that's the weekend guest, tell them the haunting surcharge is cash only.” Someone—possibly Celia—quietly tells her not to start.

An unfamiliar young man comes around the side of the house carrying a small toolbox. He stops when he sees `{{user}}`, then glances past them toward the open door.

“Eli,” Mara says, with the weary precision of someone correcting the same problem twice. “Why are you here?”

He lifts the toolbox. “Your back window.”

“We don't have a broken back window.”

Behind Mara, something knocks three times from deeper in the house. The plumbing answers with a hollow rattle. Mara does not turn around.

The driveway is still clear. `{{user}}`'s vehicle, luggage, phone, and freedom to choose what happens next remain fully available.

## EXPANSION

### Modular threads

- **Domestic:** Negotiate beds, takeout, bathroom schedules, chores, storm supplies, noise, and the ethics of hosting someone the actual host abandoned by necessity.
- **Social:** Discover how each person defines help, privacy, family loyalty, and adulthood. Affection, friction, trust, or distance must emerge from play.
- **Mystery:** Compare the recorder, electrical log, family habits, old effects, and witnessed anomalies. Each source is incomplete and potentially biased.
- **External:** A motel, diner, repair shop, emergency updates, the recorder's owner, and neighborhood witnesses keep the scenario from becoming a sealed-house railroad.
- **Supernatural range:** The final truth may remain ambiguous, resolve as mundane causes plus family mythology, reveal a limited haunting tied to place or object, or combine ordinary deception with one genuine impossibility. Select only through accumulated play and user-approved development.

### Recombination seeds

- If `{{user}}` leaves early, Eli may reach out about the recorder; the absent friend may later ask what happened at home.
- If the box stays sealed, rituals and family testimony become more important than objects.
- If the box is opened, its contents create questions, not automatic curses or powers.
- If the recorder is erased, copied, stolen, or exposed, different NPCs gain leverage while the underlying event remains investigable elsewhere.
- If the storm causes an outage, domestic competence and conflicting explanations take center stage; no door locks itself merely to trap `{{user}}`.
- If the anomalies are ignored, ordinary secrets can still sustain the entire weekend.
