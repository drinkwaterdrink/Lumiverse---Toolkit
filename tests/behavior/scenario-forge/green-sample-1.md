### CORE

**Working title:** *Three Nights at the Vale House*

**Mode:** RICH SANDBOX — grounded domestic slice of life with awkward hospitality, layered humor, and a faint supernatural edge that can remain ambiguous.

After a four-hour drive, `{{user}}` reaches an old friend’s crowded family home for a three-day weekend. A genuine family emergency has pulled the friend away just before the visit, leaving the family to host someone they barely know. The house is already over capacity: its air conditioner has failed during a warm spell, sleeping arrangements are improvised, everyone has a different idea of hospitality, and an eighteen-year-old neighbor keeps appearing with increasingly flimsy reasons to help.

There is no main quest. The weekend can revolve around meals, repairs, errands, private conversations, household friction, neighborhood interruptions, or the barely perceptible oddity of a house that sometimes seems to anticipate what its occupants need. Ordinary life remains fully playable if the supernatural thread is ignored or resolves mundanely.

**Canon boundary:** The trip, absent friend, three-day duration, household roles, adult status, persistent eighteen-year-old neighbor, grounded tone, and agency limits come from the user and are canon. All names, personalities, house details, conflicts, secrets, and supernatural possibilities below are provisional draft material until approved.

### NPC

All names and characterization are provisional.

**Carlos Vale — the absent friend, 27**

- **Goal:** Manage the family emergency without making the abandoned weekend visit feel like `{{user}}`’s problem.
- **Pressure:** His messages arrive irregularly, and he gives honest but incomplete estimates because the situation keeps changing.
- **Behavior:** Sends practical apologies, voice notes, and occasional household instructions; does not orchestrate anyone’s social life from afar.
- **Knowledge:** Knows his family’s habits and that the hallway cupboard sometimes opens on its own. He considers the latter a warped latch.
- **Uncertainty:** He cannot promise an early return.
- **Independent motion:** He may ask a relative to retrieve something he forgot, relay new travel information, or arrange another place for `{{user}}` if requested and feasible.

**Maribel Vale — Carlos’s mother, 52**

- **Goal:** Make the visit feel properly hosted despite the broken air conditioner, crowded rooms, and Carlos’s absence.
- **Pressure:** Pride makes her offer more food, bedding, and activity than the household can comfortably support.
- **Behavior:** Warm but oblique; turns apologies into tasks, labels leftovers with oddly specific instructions, and uses dry practical jokes to puncture tension.
- **Knowledge:** Knows every ordinary flaw in the house. She also knows that misplaced household objects have reappeared in the front hall for years, though she calls it “family traffic.”
- **Uncertainty:** She does not know whether the aunt has returned merely to help or to discuss selling her share of inherited family property.
- **Independent motion:** Reorganizes sleeping arrangements, attempts a complicated dinner, negotiates the repair appointment, and may privately challenge the aunt about her intentions.

**Valeria Vale — older sister, 25**

- **Goal:** Finish a remote certification project before Monday while protecting the only quiet corner of the house.
- **Pressure:** Her temporary workspace occupies the dining table everyone else needs.
- **Behavior:** Precise, understated, and funny in the form of mock regulations posted around the house; dislikes being volunteered but often helps after setting terms.
- **Knowledge:** Knows the aunt has been photographing old documents. She suspects a property dispute, not anything supernatural.
- **Uncertainty:** She cannot tell whether the soft tapping in the wall is plumbing, an animal, or the neighbor’s amateur repair work.
- **Independent motion:** Moves her work setup, questions the aunt, tests the electrical outlets, or retreats to a late-night laundromat with Wi-Fi.

**Daniela Vale — younger sister, 21**

- **Goal:** Keep the weekend from becoming solemn and extract usable audio for an eccentric local-history podcast she may never publish.
- **Pressure:** Her jokes can expose real grievances at inconvenient moments.
- **Behavior:** Social, observant, and cheerfully macabre; gives household objects funeral speeches, invents scandalous biographies for thrift-store portraits, and stops when someone clearly sets a boundary.
- **Knowledge:** Heard a voice on an old microcassette say a sentence recorded hours later, but she has not told the family because she suspects accidental overwrite or a prank.
- **Uncertainty:** She does not know who had access to the recorder.
- **Independent motion:** Interviews the aunt, recruits the neighbor for a harmless recording experiment, searches for fresh batteries, or abandons the mystery to pursue a neighborhood rumor.

**Sheryl Dane — Maribel’s sister, 46**

- **Goal:** Sort several boxes from their late mother’s storage unit before deciding what to keep, donate, or discuss with Maribel.
- **Pressure:** She arrived with little warning and avoids explaining why one box matters more than the rest.
- **Behavior:** Calm, idiosyncratically formal, capable of abrupt tenderness, and prone to repairing broken objects during difficult conversations.
- **Knowledge:** Knows one box contains their mother’s household notebooks and a sealed envelope addressed only “For whoever is here when the house gets crowded.” She has not opened it.
- **Uncertainty:** She does not know whether the envelope is a joke, practical instruction, or something personal.
- **Independent motion:** Catalogs the boxes, proposes a donation run, repairs a fan, speaks privately with Maribel, or moves the sealed envelope somewhere safer.

**Eli Mercer — neighbor, 18**

- **Goal:** Be treated as useful and grown rather than as the child from next door.
- **Pressure:** Frequent visits make even sincere help look like an excuse to linger.
- **Behavior:** Arrives with borrowed tools, returned containers, neighborhood news, or a solution that is almost correct; talks rapidly when challenged and backs off from explicit boundaries.
- **Knowledge:** Knows the outside breaker has been unreliable and that the Vale porch light sometimes turns on while the house is empty. He once saw Sheryl near the storage shed but does not know why she was there.
- **Uncertainty:** He cannot distinguish electrical faults from Daniela’s pranks.
- **Independent motion:** Attempts a fan repair, fetches a part, leaves when dismissed, talks with someone through the fence, or discovers that one of his borrowed tools has returned to the Vale hall table.

### CONFLICT

**Pressure 1 — Hospitality under load**

The house has too many people, too little cool air, contested surfaces, and shifting sleeping arrangements. Maribel keeps expanding her hosting plans while Valeria needs quiet, Daniela wants activity, Sheryl needs floor space for boxes, and Eli offers disruptive help.

- **Visible signals:** Box fans migrate between rooms; labels appear on food and outlets; bedding changes hands; the dining table repeatedly changes purpose.
- **Clock:** Advances when heat persists, meals are delayed, or shared spaces are claimed without negotiation.
- **Stages:** Good-natured improvisation → overlapping requests → a practical argument → someone creates a new arrangement or temporarily leaves the house.
- **Consequences:** Plans, room access, and NPC availability change. No response is required from `{{user}}`.
- **De-escalation:** Takeout, a library trip, a repaired fan, explicit boundaries, or letting one plan fail harmlessly.

**Pressure 2 — The boxes and the unasked question**

Sheryl’s storage boxes contain family records, ordinary clutter, and the sealed envelope. Valeria suspects a property issue; Maribel suspects Sheryl is withholding a decision; Daniela sees material for a story.

- **Visible signals:** Sheryl inventories selectively, old photographs are facedown, and one box is moved whenever the room becomes busy.
- **Concealed information:** Only Sheryl knows about the envelope at first. Maribel knows their mother sometimes wrote theatrical “future instructions,” but not that one is present.
- **Discovery routes:** Sheryl may disclose it; someone may notice the labeled envelope when the box is moved; the notebook index may reference it. None guarantees that `{{user}}` discovers or reads it.
- **Clock:** Advances when the boxes must be moved, donation plans become imminent, or the sisters directly discuss inheritance.
- **Consequences:** The envelope may be opened by its holder, relocated, dismissed, or left sealed. Its contents can be mundane, emotionally significant, teasingly coincidental, or unexplained.
- **De-escalation:** Agree to postpone the decision, store the box elsewhere, or treat the weekend as the wrong time for family business.

**Pressure 3 — Useful neighbor, unwanted frequency**

Eli’s tools and local knowledge could solve real problems, but his repeated appearances test different household boundaries.

- **Visible signals:** A knock follows almost every new household problem; his reasons are plausible but increasingly specific.
- **Knowledge split:** Eli knows about the breaker and porch light. Valeria suspects he causes some faults. Daniela thinks his timing is funny enough to encourage. Maribel sees him as a neighbor who needs firmer manners.
- **Clock:** Advances only when the household accepts further help, leaves a repair unfinished, or new errands create openings for another visit.
- **Consequences:** Eli becomes temporarily useful, gets sent home, learns a boundary, or redirects his attention to a neighborhood issue.
- **De-escalation:** A clear visiting rule, returning all borrowed items, using a professional repair service, or simply not answering the door.

**Pressure 4 — The house’s helpful timing**

Small conveniences occur just early enough to seem wrong: a needed object appears on the hall table, the porch light responds to a conversation about darkness, or Daniela’s recorder contains an impossible-seeming fragment. Every incident has at least one mundane explanation, and none establishes a supernatural truth.

- **Known:** Maribel has noticed returned objects; Carlos blames the cupboard latch; Daniela has the strange recording; Eli has seen the porch light.
- **Not known:** No one knows whether the events share a cause. Sheryl’s envelope does not automatically explain them.
- **Discovery routes:** Compare timestamps and household movements; inspect wiring and latches; ask individual witnesses; reproduce Daniela’s recording conditions; leave a controlled object and check it later.
- **Clock:** Advances when objects are repeatedly misplaced, the electrical load changes, the recorder is used, or the sealed envelope is moved—not because investigation is refused.
- **Stages:** Coincidence → repeatable pattern or plausible fault → mutually incompatible accounts → either a mundane resolution, continuing ambiguity, or one quiet event with no easy explanation.
- **Consequences:** NPC beliefs change independently. The house does not target, choose, reward, or punish `{{user}}`.
- **De-escalation:** Repair the latch and breaker, identify a prank or memory error, stop testing, or accept uncertainty.

### OPENING

The front door stands open behind a battered box fan, its cord stretched across the threshold beneath a strip of fluorescent orange tape. Warm air carries the mixed smell of toasted cumin, laundry detergent, and an overheated power strip.

Maribel holds a phone between her shoulder and ear while directing Sheryl and Daniela around three mismatched stacks of bedding. “Carlos is safe,” she says as soon as the call ends. “Still gone all weekend. He is extremely sorry, which is convenient because he is not here to perform the emotion.”

From the dining room, Valeria raises one hand without looking away from a laptop. A paper sign taped to the chair reads **TEMPORARY MUNICIPAL SILENCE DISTRICT**, followed by six footnotes in smaller print.

Someone knocks on the already open door.

Eli waits on the porch with a toolbox in one hand and a bag of ice in the other. “I heard the breaker clicked again,” he says. His attention shifts to the box fan. “Also, that model was recalled. Maybe. I can check.”

At the same moment, Daniela lifts a microcassette recorder from the hall table. “Did anybody put this here?”

Sheryl stops moving a storage box. Maribel looks from Eli to the recorder to the fan cord and then addresses `{{user}}` for the first time since the call ended.

“There are cold drinks in the kitchen, one room with a door that mostly closes, and absolutely no obligation to participate in whatever this becomes. What do you need first?”

### EXPANSION

**Modular scene engines**

- **Dinner by incompatible methods:** If Maribel insists on cooking through the heat, each NPC solves a different piece of the meal, exposing habits and frictions. If the plan is abandoned, the takeout order or store trip creates its own conversations and absences.
- **The fan repair:** If anyone accepts Eli’s help, the repair can reveal an ordinary wiring fault, a tool already waiting where it should not be, or both. If refused, Eli may leave the bag of ice and go.
- **Donation run:** If Sheryl moves the boxes, characters divide between the house, vehicle, storage site, and thrift shop. The envelope’s location changes only through an established action.
- **Quiet-hours experiment:** If Daniela tests the recorder, participants choose what to record and who handles it. Results may be contamination, a family prank, an audio artifact, or one phrase whose timing remains unexplained.
- **Work deadline:** If Valeria loses the dining table or internet, she negotiates, relocates, or misses a self-imposed milestone. This pressure matters even if no mystery is pursued.
- **Emergency updates:** Carlos’s messages can change practical plans without revealing a false second crisis. If `{{user}}` wants another arrangement, he and the family make a realistic attempt to provide one.

**Conditional branches**

- If household boundaries are stated clearly, NPCs respond according to personality: Maribel may overcorrect, Daniela may make a single joke and comply, Eli may become briefly defensive before leaving, and Sheryl may quietly enforce the boundary later.
- If the sealed envelope is opened by someone with access, choose its contents to fit the established tone: mundane household instructions, an affectionate family provocation, or language that appears coincidentally relevant. It never names `{{user}}` or assigns them a role.
- If anomalies are investigated, preserve multiple explanations until play earns stronger evidence. If they are ignored, the weekend continues through meals, errands, work, gossip, and family decisions.
- If tensions rise, allow retreat, apology, a change of venue, practical compromise, or lasting disagreement. Reconciliation is possible but never automatic.
- If `{{user}}` spends time with any NPC, familiarity develops only from what occurs in play; it does not imply trust, intimacy, attraction, or romance.
