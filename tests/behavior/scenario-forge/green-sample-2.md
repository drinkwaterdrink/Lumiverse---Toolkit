### CORE

**Scenario title:** *The House That Keeps a Spare Key*

**Mode:** RICH SANDBOX

After a four-hour drive, `{{user}}` reaches the crowded Mercer family home for a planned three-day weekend with an old friend—only to learn that the friend, Devin, has already left for a genuine out-of-town family emergency. Devin cannot return before Monday. The Mercers still honor the invitation, leaving `{{user}}` in a busy house where hospitality is sincere, privacy is scarce, and every ordinary inconvenience exposes a different family fault line.

The weekend runs on domestic pressures rather than a main quest: a failing kitchen sink, disputed sleeping space, an overworked bathroom, a neighborhood yard sale, Devin's occasional logistical calls, and an 18-year-old neighbor who can produce a practical excuse to visit at nearly any hour. Beneath that sits one faint anomaly. The old house sometimes gives three precise knocks shortly before something is found, fails, or changes location. Every incident has a plausible mundane explanation—pipes, warped floors, vibration, absent-minded residents—and the scenario never requires a supernatural conclusion.

**Tone:** grounded slice of life; awkward but non-hostile hospitality; layered, character-specific humor; crowded-house intimacy; very slow ambiguity. Mundane play must remain satisfying even if the anomaly is ignored or explained away.

**Canon boundary:** The trip, three-day duration, absent old friend, genuine emergency, household roles, neighbor's age and visiting habit, adult cast, requested tone, and agency limits are user-supplied canon. All names, occupations, house details, secrets, conflicts, and supernatural possibilities below are **provisional** until approved. Whether the knocks are supernatural is deliberately **unresolved**.

### NPC

All generated NPC details are provisional. Initial reactions to `{{user}}` are practical and undecided; rapport, friction, trust, and affection must arise through play.

**Devin Mercer, 27 — the absent old friend**

- **Goal:** Handle the genuine family emergency without leaving either the household or `{{user}}` stranded.
- **Pressure:** Poor reception, a stressed relative, and guilt about the timing make Devin's messages brief and uneven.
- **Behavior:** Sends concrete updates, apologizes once rather than constantly, and calls family members separately when logistics matter.
- **Knowledge:** Knows the family's broad habits and that the house makes strange noises, but considers the knocking a running household joke.

- **Independent motion:** Devin may request a photograph of a document or ask a relative—not necessarily `{{user}}`—to find it. The emergency remains genuine and does not become a mystery bait-and-switch.

**Rina Mercer, 52 — Devin's mother**

- **Goal:** Keep the weekend functional and fulfill Devin's invitation without performing false intimacy.
- **Pressure:** A plumber has postponed twice, her small repair fund is nearly spent, and she dislikes letting the family see her worry.
- **Behavior:** Labels leftovers with dates and increasingly specific warnings; expresses care through towels, food, spare chargers, and blunt logistical questions.
- **Humor:** Treats household failures like underperforming employees: “The sink is on a corrective-action plan.”
- **Knowledge:** Knows the pipes knock, knows the repair budget is thin, and knows the neighbor sometimes comes over to avoid tension at home.
- **Uncertainty:** Does not know why a few objects have turned up in the closed serving drawer.
- **Independent motion:** Calls repair companies, reorganizes sleeping space, and quietly prices a replacement fixture whether or not `{{user}}` participates.

**Mae Mercer, 29 — older sister, overnight emergency dispatcher**

- **Goal:** Sleep before her next shift and recover a missing work badge without creating a family search party.
- **Pressure:** The den was supposed to be quiet, but it is now part guest room, part storage area, and part route to the back door.

- **Humor:** Gives harmless domestic events incident codes and describes bad leftovers as “evidence pending disposal.”

**Tamsin Mercer, 23 — younger sister, appliance reseller and audio hobbyist**

- **Pressure:** Rina wants the table cleared; Mae blames her for encouraging the house legend; the deck intermittently records sounds that were not obvious in the room.
- **Behavior:** Names appliances only after they fail twice, barters favors instead of asking directly, and records room tone to diagnose motors and wiring.

**Celia Voss, 46 — Rina's younger sister, temporarily staying in the house**

- **Goal:** Finish restoring a damaged miniature house for a museum contract while keeping the uncertain length of her stay private.
- **Pressure:** Her apartment suffered a real ceiling leak; repairs may take longer than she has admitted. Her boxes occupy the linen closet and part of Devin's room.

**Noah Bell, 18 — the neighbor with endless errands**

- **Knowledge:** Knows the exterior wall beside the serving drawer contains an old capped vent. He once heard knocking from outside while nobody was visibly near it.
- **Uncertainty:** Does not know about Celia's ledger or the recordings. His reasons for visiting are social refuge and familiarity, not automatic attraction to anyone.
- **Independent motion:** Helps with the yard sale, comes and goes, responds to events at his own home, and may stop visiting if repeatedly treated as a nuisance.

### CONFLICT

The scenario uses four pressures that can advance independently and recombine without becoming a single plot.

**1. Guest-space arithmetic**

- **Entry condition:** `{{user}}` needs a place to stay while Celia's boxes occupy part of Devin's room and Mae needs daytime quiet near the den.
- **Active goals:** Rina wants a workable arrangement; Mae wants sleep; Celia wants her boxes left undisturbed; Noah offers a folding cot partly to be useful.
- **Clock:** Advances when rooms are reassigned, noise accumulates, or nobody addresses access. Stages: awkward improvisation → competing needs → someone voluntarily changes plans or an alternate arrangement is made.
- **Consequences:** Fatigue, clutter, schedule changes, or bruised patience. It never forces `{{user}}` to remain in the house or accept a particular bed.
- **De-escalation:** Clear boundaries, a quiet-hours agreement, moving only approved boxes, another lodging choice, or Mae using a coworker's spare room.

**2. The sink, the sale, and the shrinking repair fund**

- **Entry condition:** The kitchen sink leaks while the family prepares items for Sunday's yard sale.
- **Active goals:** Rina wants a cheap repair; Tamsin wants her cassette deck ready to sell; Celia needs dry workspace; Mae wants the noise controlled.
- **Hidden fact:** Rina's repair reserve is almost gone. Rina knows; Mae suspects; the others do not.
- **Discovery routes:** A visible estimate or returned payment envelope; a direct conversation; overhearing Rina's end of a plumber call.
- **Clock:** Advances only with water use, a failed patch, new expenses, or approaching sale time. Stages: drip → cabinet damage risk → water shutoff or paid repair.
- **Consequences:** Changed meal plans, a trip for parts, rearranged workspaces, or choosing what to sell. The anomaly is not required.

**3. Noah's open-door habit**

- **Entry condition:** Noah arrives with a plausible errand and remains if invited.
- **Active goals:** Noah wants company and usefulness; Rina wants boundaries without rejecting him; Tamsin values his help; Mae needs quiet.
- **Hidden fact:** His own household is strained, but not dangerous by default. Noah knows the full reason; Rina knows only that home is difficult; Mae has noticed the pattern.
- **Discovery routes:** Noah chooses to explain; a phone call changes his demeanor; his visits stop when the situation at home changes; a family member mentions it without giving private details.
- **Clock:** Advances through repeated invitations, explicit boundaries, or events at Noah's home—not merely because scenes need drama.
- **Consequences:** He visits less, becomes a steadier helper, redirects to the garage, or returns home. No romance or hostility is presumed.

**4. Three knocks and the good drawer**

- **Entry condition:** Three distinct knocks occur near a mundane change: a leak worsens, an object is found, a door shifts, or the cassette deck starts recording.
- **Visible signals:** Audible knocks; Mae's timestamps; Tamsin's recordings; the capped exterior vent; small misplaced objects found in the serving drawer.
- **Concealed information:** Celia has the old maintenance ledger. Celia alone knows this; no one knows whether its stranger entries are factual.
- **Discovery routes:** Celia voluntarily reveals it; someone notices the loose closet panel and asks; the ledger becomes relevant during box-moving. The audio can be found through Tamsin's tests or comparison with Mae's timestamps.
- **Clock:** Advances only when another qualifying incident occurs or evidence is actively compared. Stages: dismissible noise → repeated pattern → contradictory mundane evidence → unresolved choice between explanations.
- **Consequences:** Residents alter routines, test pipes, search the vent, joke more carefully, or decide coincidence is enough. There is no attack, prophecy, possession, chosen observer, or mandatory reveal.
- **Mundane resolutions:** Water hammer, duct vibration, household members relocating objects, overlapping recordings, or an old reminder ritual copied unconsciously.
- **Strange resolution, if later chosen:** The house does not communicate in words; it nudges unattended things toward a historically designated drawer before practical trouble. It has no special bond with `{{user}}`.

### OPENING

The Mercer house sits at the end of a short, sun-softened driveway, its front windows open despite the rattle of an overworked box fan. Devin's last message remains on the phone: the emergency is real, reception is poor, and Rina knows about the visit. A second message contains only, *I am so sorry. Please don't let them turn this into a committee.*

The porch door opens before the engine heat has finished ticking. Rina Mercer stands behind the screen with a stack of folded towels under one arm and a screwdriver in the other. She studies the unfamiliar car, then `{{user}}`, with neither warmth nor suspicion settled yet.

“You made the drive. Good. Devin made the emergency. Bad. The invitation still stands, but the sleeping arrangement has entered litigation.”

From somewhere inside, a woman calls, “I withdrew my objection at two fourteen.”

Another voice answers, “You labeled the couch a biohazard.”

“That was advisory.”

Rina closes her eyes for half a second. Behind her, the house is dense with ordinary evidence of too many adults sharing too few surfaces: museum-sized boxes along one wall, a dismantled cassette deck on the dining table, yard-sale stickers on a lamp, and a mixing bowl positioned beneath the kitchen plumbing.

Three neat knocks sound from inside the wall.

Rina's eyes open. “Pipes.”

“Water's off,” says the younger voice from the dining room.

At the side gate, Noah appears carrying a folded camping cot and an extension cord, as if summoned by the argument. “Delivery,” he announces. “Strictly logistical. I have no social motive.”

Rina shifts the towels, the screwdriver, and the weight of five simultaneous problems. “Before this house introduces itself badly: do you want the room situation, the plumbing situation, or the people situation explained first?”

### EXPANSION

**Modular scene engines**

- **Kitchen triage:** If the leak worsens, any available character may propose a patch, a hardware-store trip, washing dishes elsewhere, or shutting off the water. Success changes the practical state; it does not unlock the anomaly.
- **The yard-sale economy:** If Sunday approaches, each NPC chooses what to sell, rescue, repair, or secretly remove from the sale. Objects can expose history without requiring a grand revelation.
- **Quiet-hours negotiation:** If Mae cannot sleep, the household must redistribute noise, space, or expectations. Mae may solve it herself by leaving; nobody must mediate.
- **Noah at the threshold:** Each visit can be accepted, redirected, shortened, or refused. His behavior changes from accumulated boundaries rather than a single approval score.
- **The recording test:** If Tamsin preserves the tape, comparing it with plumbing, outdoor noise, and Mae's timestamps can strengthen either mundane or strange explanations.
- **Celia's miniature:** If someone takes interest, the damaged model reveals Celia's patience, finances, and reluctance to discuss her housing situation. It is not a magical replica unless a later project decision deliberately changes the supernatural level.

**Callbacks and state changes**

- If an object is deliberately placed in the serving drawer, record who placed it and why; do not later present it as anomalous.
- If someone learns Rina's financial situation, track exactly who knows and whether Rina knows they know.
- If Noah discloses why he visits, his future appearances become less theatrical only if the interaction gives him reason to change.
- If the sink is repaired, keep it repaired. Future knocks require a different plausible source.
- If Celia reveals the ledger, it becomes shared evidence only for those present or later told.
- Any bond, resentment, joke, boundary, promise, or attraction develops only through played interaction and applies to the people involved, never the whole household by default.

**Conditional escalation**

- If two knock incidents occur under controlled conditions, the family divides over whether further testing is worthwhile; no conclusion is forced.
- If the yard sale moves the old serving cabinet, a capped vent and pencil maintenance marks become visible. They may explain the sound without explaining the drawer.
- If the house remains crowded and boundaries stay unclear, characters independently seek space: back steps, garage, diner, library, work, or another house.
- If Devin needs a document, the search can intersect Celia's boxes and the loose closet panel, but Devin asks a family member first unless play establishes another reason.
- If every strange hook is ignored, the weekend remains active through meals, repairs, visitors, work schedules, neighborhood exchanges, and the practical comedy of hosting a stranger.

**De-escalation and alternate paths**

- A plumber arrives early, the yard sale earns enough for repairs, or a neighbor lends a sink part.
- The knocks cease after a pipe fix, leaving the ledger as an eccentric historical artifact.
- Celia decides the ledger is private clutter and replaces it; the mystery can end unresolved.
- `{{user}}` may relocate, shorten the stay, or keep the household at polite distance while the NPCs continue their own weekend.

**Alternate entry points**

- Saturday breakfast after a first night handled offscreen, with the sink cabinet emptied across the kitchen.
- The hardware-store parking lot, where two incompatible repair plans compete.
- Sunday's yard sale, when household objects and neighborhood visitors bring multiple small histories into one space.
- A late-night common-room scene where Mae is leaving for work, Tamsin is testing audio, and Celia is quietly returning something to the linen closet.

**Lorebook growth flags**

- Mercer house layout, access points, noise paths, and the serving drawer.
- Household routines, quiet hours, food-label system, repair ledger, and yard-sale customs.
- Individual NPC knowledge states, schedules, boundaries, and independently earned relationship changes.
- Noah's household context, kept proportionate and private unless play develops it.
- Anomaly evidence ledger separating observed fact, witness report, mundane hypothesis, disproven explanation, and unresolved possibility.
