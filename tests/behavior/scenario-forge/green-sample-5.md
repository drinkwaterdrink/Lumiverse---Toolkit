## CORE

**Working title:** *The Long Weekend at Bellweather House*

**Mode:** RICH SANDBOX  
**Tone:** Grounded domestic slice of life, awkward hospitality, dry and occasionally dark humor, crowded-house intimacy, and a supernatural edge faint enough to remain arguable.

**Canon boundary:** The three-day visit, genuine family emergency, four-hour drive, absent old friend, family composition, adult cast, persistent eighteen-year-old neighbor, and all restrictions concerning `{{user}}` come from the user. Names, ages beyond those supplied, the house, household history, secrets, pressures, and strange events below are **provisional playable material**, not established project canon.

Friday evening finds `{{user}}` at Bellweather House, where the promised low-key reunion has abruptly become a weekend among strangers. Old friend **Cal Mercer** has already left to help a hospitalized relative several counties away and will not return before Monday. Cal's family means to honor the invitation, but the house is overfull, its routines are oddly specific, and nobody agrees on what good hospitality looks like.

The ordinary situation can sustain the roleplay by itself: sleeping arrangements must be negotiated, the kitchen ceiling has started leaking, a family supper is half-prepared, and eighteen-year-old neighbor **Owen Pike** keeps arriving with increasingly flimsy reasons to be useful. Beneath that, Bellweather House has a minor habit nobody discusses consistently. Sounds sometimes seem to arrive from the wrong room. A small brass service bell rings without anyone near it. These incidents may be old-house acoustics, family mischief, stress, coincidence, or something stranger; the scenario never requires one explanation.

The promise of play is a weekend of shifting alliances, chores, jokes, private conversations, small boundary tests, and household mysteries. There is no main quest. Any thread may be pursued, declined, redirected, or left to change offscreen.

## NPC

All names and generated details in this section are provisional.

### Mara Mercer — Cal's mother, 52

- **Goal:** Keep Cal's promise to host `{{user}}` without letting the weekend expose how strained the household currently is.
- **Pressure:** She has volunteered the house faster than she can reorganize it and treats logistics as a moral obligation.
- **Behavior:** Warm but brisk; asks practical questions in clusters; labels leftovers with exact dates; delivers sincere concern in the tone of a building inspector.
- **Knowledge:** Knows the leak has happened twice before and that the brass bell belonged to her late mother. She believes old pipes and shifting floorboards explain most “house nonsense.”
- **Uncertainty:** She does not know why the bell was found in a sealed cupboard after she donated it last spring.
- **Independent motion:** She will reshuffle beds, call a roofer, finish dinner, and quietly ask family members not to burden the guest—even if nobody helps.

### Lena Mercer — older adult sister, 29

- **Goal:** Finish a remote-work deadline and keep one quiet room available, preferably the room everyone else has decided should become guest space.
- **Pressure:** She is the household's reliable problem-solver and resents being volunteered before anyone asks.
- **Behavior:** Precise, restrained, and unexpectedly funny when irritated; uses sticky notes as diplomacy and legal briefs as threats.
- **Knowledge:** Knows the upstairs outlet flickers when the kitchen fan runs. She once recorded a voice-like sound in the empty laundry room but deleted it as “embarrassing pipe content.”
- **Uncertainty:** She suspects Owen knows more about recent nighttime noises than he admits.
- **Independent motion:** She will test circuits, claim workspace, negotiate chores, and investigate any repeatable anomaly with mundane tools.

### Wren Mercer — younger adult sister, 23

- **Goal:** Keep the weekend from becoming tense enough that everyone retreats behind closed doors.
- **Pressure:** Her jokes often arrive a half-step past good taste, and the family cannot always tell when she is covering concern.
- **Behavior:** Inventive, observant, and casually macabre; turns chores into games, assigns dramatic names to household failures, and apologizes directly when told she crossed a line.
- **Knowledge:** Knows the bell sometimes rings after someone tells a deliberate lie in the kitchen—or at least claims to have noticed that pattern.
- **Uncertainty:** She may be teasing, testing a theory, or doing both. She does not know whether anyone has been secretly ringing it.
- **Independent motion:** She will improvise a sleeping chart, recruit Owen for repairs, test the “lying bell” theory, or abandon the joke if it genuinely upsets someone.

### Aunt Delia Rusk — Mara's adult sister, 47

- **Goal:** Sort several boxes of family papers before leaving Monday, without explaining why one particular envelope matters to her.
- **Pressure:** Her temporary stay already displaced the household before `{{user}}` arrived. She dislikes being treated as either a guest or a crisis.
- **Behavior:** Self-contained, wry, and courteous; restores broken household objects while insisting she is “only making them easier to throw away.”
- **Knowledge:** Recognizes the bell and knows the house once had a wired servant-call system. She also knows her mother sometimes used the dead wires to summon children from other rooms.
- **Uncertainty:** She cannot explain a recent handwritten note among the papers that appears newer than the notebook containing it.
- **Independent motion:** She will continue sorting, hide or relocate the envelope if she feels watched, repair part of the bell wire, and eventually confront Mara over their mother's belongings.

### Owen Pike — neighbor, 18

- **Goal:** Stay welcome at Bellweather House because it is livelier and less lonely than his own home; tonight, he also wants back a borrowed socket wrench.
- **Pressure:** He invents unnecessary errands rather than admit he wants company, making ordinary visits look suspicious.
- **Behavior:** Helpful, talkative when given a task, evasive when asked why he came over; arrives carrying an object that almost justifies the visit.
- **Knowledge:** Knows an exterior vent carries kitchen sounds toward his yard. He heard the service bell through that vent two nights ago while everyone said the kitchen was empty.
- **Uncertainty:** He does not know about the old call wires. He suspects Wren staged the ringing and worries she thinks he is gullible.
- **Independent motion:** He will retrieve tools, offer patch materials for the leak, leave if firmly dismissed, or return later with a practical new reason if the household remains receptive.

### Cal Mercer — absent old friend, adult

- **Goal:** Handle the genuine family emergency while preventing the failed reunion from becoming everyone else's problem.
- **Pressure:** Communication is intermittent, and attempts to smooth things over may create more obligations for the family.
- **Behavior:** Sends concise updates when able; does not fabricate progress or secretly return.
- **Knowledge:** Knows the household's ordinary frictions and believes the strange sounds are a long-running family joke mixed with old ductwork.
- **Uncertainty:** Does not know the ceiling has begun leaking again or that Aunt Delia found the note.

## CONFLICT

### Pressure A — The Hospitality Tangle

- **Active immediately:** The den is Lena's workroom, Delia occupies the spare room, and the couch sits beneath the new ceiling leak. No sleeping place has been assigned to `{{user}}`.
- **Competing goals:** Mara wants a comfortable solution now; Lena needs quiet; Delia refuses to displace anyone without discussion; Wren favors a communal rearrangement.
- **Visible signals:** Folded linens on three different chairs, an air mattress with no visible pump, labeled doors, and a slow drip into a stockpot.
- **Clock:** Advances when rain continues, bedtime approaches, or rooms are committed to other uses. It can pause through a repair, hotel offer, altered household arrangement, or any solution established in play.
- **Consequences:** NPC fatigue, changed room access, private conversations created or prevented, and new chore negotiations. No arrangement is imposed on `{{user}}` without a response.

### Pressure B — The Unfinished Family Sort

- **Active immediately:** Delia's boxes occupy the dining table needed for dinner.
- **Competing goals:** Delia wants privacy and speed; Mara wants the boxes returned to storage; Lena wants the table cleared; Wren wants to know what is being hidden.
- **Concealed fact:** One envelope contains a mundane but painful draft letter their mother never sent. Delia knows its contents; Mara does not; Wren suspects a secret but not its nature; Lena has no special access.
- **Discovery routes:** Delia may disclose it during earned conversation; Mara may recognize the handwriting if the envelope becomes visible; the dispute's history may emerge through separate family accounts. The document is not automatically found or read.
- **Clock:** Advances if the boxes are moved, dinner needs the table, or Delia feels her privacy has been breached.
- **Consequences:** The sisters may argue, establish firmer boundaries, postpone the sort, or resolve it without involving `{{user}}`.

### Pressure C — Owen's Revolving Errands

- **Active immediately:** Owen arrives with a misdelivered grocery bag and asks about his wrench.
- **Competing goals:** Owen wants a reason to stay; Mara welcomes useful help; Lena wants fewer interruptions; Wren finds his excuses entertaining until they become inconvenient.
- **Concealed fact:** Owen's parents are away often, and the busy Mercer house gives him ordinary company. Owen knows this; Mara partly suspects; Wren incorrectly suspects a crush is the whole explanation; Lena has not considered it.
- **Discovery routes:** Owen may admit it if asked without ridicule; his repeated preference for chores over attention may make the pattern inferable; a casual comment from Mara can supply context.
- **Clock:** Advances when Owen is included, dismissed, embarrassed, or given a real task.
- **Consequences:** He may become a useful repair hand, leave to preserve dignity, stop visiting briefly, or return for reasons unrelated to romance.

### Pressure D — The Bell in the Wall

- **Active condition:** A small brass bell rings when nobody is visibly touching it, or a sound seems to originate in the wrong room.
- **Visible signals:** One or two clean metallic notes; a frayed wire behind the pantry molding; pipes, vents, and floor vibration that provide plausible explanations.
- **Concealed information:** Delia knows about the old call system. Lena knows about her deleted recording. Owen knows what he heard from outside. Wren has the lying-bell theory. Mara knows the bell previously returned after being donated. Nobody possesses a complete explanation.
- **Discovery routes:** Inspecting the old wiring or house records; comparing separate witness accounts; testing vents and vibrations; observing when the bell rings; finding the thrift-store receipt among unsorted papers. None guarantees a supernatural conclusion.
- **Clock:** Advances only after repeat incidents, repair work on the wires, deliberate testing, severe weather, or somebody moving the bell. Ignoring it keeps the phenomena rare and peripheral.
- **Consequences:** Mundane faults may be found and fixed; family stories may surface; a test may produce contradictory evidence; the bell may remain unexplained. It never selects `{{user}}`, grants a power, or demands investigation.

## OPENING

Friday, 6:47 PM. Heat ticks from the hood of the car after the four-hour drive. Bellweather House stands at the end of a short, crowded driveway: two bicycles under the porch, a rolled rug blocking half the front hall, and a stockpot visible through the window beneath a steady ceiling drip.

Mara Mercer has the front door open before the engine finishes cooling. She holds a dish towel in one hand and Cal's call on speaker in the other.

“The emergency is real, Cal is where Cal needs to be, and you are still welcome here,” she says, with the practiced firmness of someone answering three different objections at once. “I can show you the sleeping options first, or feed you first. Neither category is currently impressive.”

From somewhere inside, Lena calls, “The den is not an option until nine.”

“The den is a concept,” Wren calls back. “Concepts cannot be reserved.”

The screen door bumps open against Owen Pike's shoulder. The eighteen-year-old neighbor is carrying a paper grocery bag with **MERCER** written across it and a socket wrench that appears to undermine his stated reason for visiting.

“Delivery mistake,” he says, then glances toward the ceiling drip. “Also, I have roof patch. Not with me. But nearby. In my house. Where roof patch traditionally lives.”

Behind Mara, a brass bell gives two neat rings from the empty kitchen.

Everyone pauses for a different length of time.

Mara shifts the phone away from the sound. “Food, beds, leak, or whatever performance that was,” she says. “We can start anywhere.”

## EXPANSION

### Modular branches

- **If practical help takes priority:** The leak becomes a cooperative scene with competing repair methods, room access, and opportunities for ordinary conversation. Successful mitigation slows the hospitality clock but does not solve the family-paper dispute.
- **If accommodations take priority:** Each proposed space reveals a different household routine and boundary. Any accepted arrangement updates room access and nighttime presence; any declined arrangement remains declined unless circumstances materially change.
- **If dinner takes priority:** The boxes must be moved, incorporated into the seating chaos, or left where they are. Conversation can expose conflicting family recollections without forcing disclosure of Delia's letter.
- **If Owen is welcomed:** Give him a concrete task and let usefulness, awkwardness, or overstaying emerge through behavior. Friendship, irritation, attraction, or distrust are never automatic for anyone, especially `{{user}}`.
- **If Owen is dismissed:** He leaves. Later consequences are mundane—unfinished repairs, bruised pride, or less information about the vent—not punishment for refusing a hook.
- **If the bell is investigated:** Begin with physical causes. Each test should produce information rather than a guaranteed answer; supernatural intensity rises only if the user continues engaging and the evidence survives mundane explanations.
- **If the bell is ignored:** Keep it to one optional background recurrence at most. The weekend remains fully playable through meals, repairs, boundaries, errands, and family tensions.
- **If the family-paper dispute escalates:** Let the Mercers handle their own conflict unless `{{user}}` voluntarily participates. Possible outcomes include disclosure, postponement, apology, a new storage boundary, or Delia leaving early.
